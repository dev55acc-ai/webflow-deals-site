Webflow Template Licensing: What a $79 Purchase Actually Gets You

WebflowDeals editorial · Updated September 19, 2026 · ~5 min read

The least-read page before a template purchase is the license, and it's the one that bites. A marketplace template costs the same whether you use it once or twelve times — the license is what makes those two uses very different purchases. Here's what the Webflow marketplace license actually grants, in plain language, before you click buy.

The core rule: one live website per purchase

Every purchase on the Webflow marketplace — free or paid — licenses the template for one live project. You can move it, extend it, and rebuild it inside that single site, but you cannot reskin the same base and publish it as a second site without buying a second copy.

This is the clause that quietly costs people money. Redesigning a client site with "your" template under a second Webflow project is a second use. Duplicating a paid template you once bought to spin up a side project is a second use. If you plan to do either, bake the cost of extra licenses into the project quote, not the surprise budget.

It doesn't matter for the free Starter plan — until it does

Free templates carry the same license, but you can run them under the free Starter plan for as long as you like, on your webflow.io subdomain, before paying anything. The license becomes a real purchase only when the site goes live on a custom domain — which requires a paid site plan. That's the cheapest way in: build and customize on Starter, buy the plan the day you connect your own domain. Most buyers who treat this as the flow end up paying for one month of hosting, not one year of early indecision. If you're still deciding which plan, our Webflow pricing guide walks the decision.

Client work: one client, one license

Buying a template and building a client's site on it is allowed — the final website is yours (or the client's) to sell. What you can't do is buy once and reuse the base across several client projects. For agencies and freelancers who build on templates regularly, the honest math is: one license per delivered site that starts from a bought template. If you'd rather not carry that cost, build on top of your own system or a free template instead — plenty of studios ship beautiful sites off the free category (see the free picks in our September template roundup).

How to check a specific template before you buy

  1. Open the template's page and read its own license note — some authors add terms beyond the marketplace default.
  2. List every image and font the demo uses; then check the template's asset documentation for what you must replace.
  3. Check the author's update history. A maintained template stays compatible with the current Webflow feature set; a one-off from years ago may carry outdated structure.
  4. Open the live preview and run the six-step buying checklist — it applies to every category, not just portfolios.
The bottom line: a template purchase is one license for one live site, and almost always needs you to supply your own images and fonts. Plan for those two costs and the surprise budget disappears. Price-tag shock is the $79; the license and asset gaps are the part that actually costs.
