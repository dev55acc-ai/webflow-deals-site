WebflowDeals editorial · Updated September 17, 2026 · ~5 min read

Webflow's pricing page is a wall of feature grids, and the features that actually gate your launch are easy to miss. Here's the current structure — pulled from the live pricing page in September 2026 — and a decision flow for which plan to buy, if any.

All plans include Webflow AI, custom code, the global CDN, GSAP-powered interactions, and the new E-commerce-free (see below) hosting stack. Prices are USD, per site, plus applicable taxes, and billing yearly is where the $15/$25 numbers come from — the page advertises up to 40% saved on yearly versus the monthly toggle.

The three traps that cost money

  1. Starter cannot publish to a custom domain. You can build and customize for free forever, but the moment you want your own domain (i.e. the moment anyone treats the site as real), you're on Basic or Premium.
  2. CMS is a Premium thing. Basic gets you a beautiful static site — but the moment you want a blog governed by content collections, scheduled publishing, 20,000 CMS items, or collection-based templates (which is most portfolio and agency sites), the requirement floats you to Premium.
  3. Bandwidth climbs fast on the cheap plan. Basic's 10 GB/month is roughly 3,000–5,000 average page loads. Great for a business-card site; a content site with a viral post will blow through it and Webflow automatically upgrades you after two consecutive over-limit months.

E-commerce is separate

Online stores don't run on Basic or Premium — Webflow prices E-commerce plans separately from the site plans above. If a template collects more than ~50 products or needs checkout logic, budget for a dedicated E-commerce plan rather than assuming Premium covers it.

Which plan should you buy?

  1. Testing an idea, one page, no domain needed → Starter (free). Build, break, restart. Pay nothing.
  2. Business site without a blog/CMS engine → Basic ($15/mo yearly). Custom domain, unlimited forms, badge gone.
  3. Portfolio, agency, blog, or any CMS-driven site → Premium ($25/mo yearly). The 40/20,000 CMS headroom and page-level control are what portfolios actually grow into.
  4. Online store → separate E-commerce plan; compare before committing to a template.
The money-saving move: build and customize on the free Starter plan, pick your template, and only buy Basic/Premium the day you're ready to connect a custom domain. The Designer is free — hosting is what costs. And if you're choosing a template, run it through our portfolio template checklist before committing to a plan.
