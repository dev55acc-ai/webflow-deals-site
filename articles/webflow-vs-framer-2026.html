Webflow vs Framer in 2026: Template Ecosystem, Real Costs, and Which to Pick

WebflowDeals editorial · Updated September 20, 2026 · ~6 min read

Every founder tends to compare Webflow and Framer on the same two things the marketing pages want them to compare: design freedom and AI. Those are rarely the deciders. What actually decides your project is the template ecosystem you're buying into, the hosting limits under real traffic, and what it costs to go from a free trial to a live custom-domain site. Here's the practical head-to-head.

Where the money actually goes

Webflow's free Starter tier is generous for building: you can design, customize, and test a template for 30 days without paying. The catch is the same one we cover in the full pricing guide — publishing to a custom domain starts at $15/mo yearly (Basic), and the moment you want a CMS-driven blog you're on Premium at $25/mo.

Framer's entry point is cheaper on paper and its yearly Mini/mini-tier undercuts Webflow's Basic. But Framer's content model is simpler, its native e-commerce is absent (you bolt on Stripe/Shopify integrations), and its template library skews toward landing pages rather than CMS-heavy business sites. Cheap is only cheap if the features you need exist on that plan.

Which ecosystem has the better templates?

  1. Webflow marketplace — broader and deeper per category. Portfolio, agency, e-commerce, blog, SaaS: you'll find several credible options per category with real CMS setups. License model is one live site per template purchase (details in our licensing explainer), so budget per site, not once.
  2. Framer templates — noticeably modern, and if you're coming from Figma the mental model is nearly identical. Where Webflow templates expect you to learn columns/classes, Framer templates mostly just edit. The trade-off is the cap on template richness: fewer deep CMS/collection patterns per purchase.

If you already know which category you need, start from the marketplace — see our portfolio template checklist for the buyer's traps that transfer to both platforms.

Hosting and the "it went viral" test

Both sites are static-generated on a CDN, which is why both feel fast. The real difference is the plan ceiling:

  • Webflow Basic ships 10 GB/mo (~3,000–5,000 page loads). A portfolio or agency site is fine; a content site with one popular post will approach the ceiling and Webflow auto-upgrades after repeated overages.
  • Framer pages are typically lighter (fully generated markup, fewer kilograms of CMS tooling), so the same traffic burns less of a plan's allowance.

Rule of thumb: if you expect sustained traffic, price the plan up on whichever platform — not the reverse. The "cheap" tier is for starters, not for launches you're betting on.

Decision flow

  1. You're a Figma native, only need a killer marketing/landing site → Framer. Fastest path from draft to custom-domain launch.
  2. You need a blog, portfolio, agency site, or CMS-driven content → Webflow. The collection-based CMS and marketplace depth are the point.
  3. You're building an online store → Webflow's dedicated E-commerce plans, and check the template licensing before you commit.
  4. You're unsure → build the same page free in both for a day. The tool that feels right to you beats the benchmark every time — but remember to cost the published site, not the trial.
Bottom line: this isn't "which is better" — it's which plan and template library matches your project type. Picking the platform before naming the project type is how founders end up migrating mid-launch. If you're leaning Webflow, read the plan-by-plan breakdown next, then choose the template.
