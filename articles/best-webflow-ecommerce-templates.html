Best Webflow Ecommerce Templates: The 2026 Buyers Guide

An ecommerce template costs more than a brochure template — and so does the plan that publishes it. Here's what to verify in the demo before you spend, which template features actually matter for a store, and the license traps that quietly cost a second purchase.

WebflowDeals editorial · Published September 23, 2026 · ~5 min read

First: know the plan requirement before you pick a template

Webflow's free Starter plan can't publish to a live domain. To run a storefront on a custom domain you need a paid Site plan, and ecommerce functionality sits on top of that as an Ecommerce plan. The template is the least flexible part of the stack — it can be swapped later, but only at the cost of rebuilding your design. So the plan question comes first, the template second.

Practical rule: if you're choosing between templates, you must already be planning to pay for a Site plan. Shopping templates with the free plan in mind is how buyers buy twice.

The 8-point checklist for any ecommerce template

Walk the live demo — not the designer preview — and check each of these before you buy the license:

1. Product pages. Does the demo show a real product page with images, a description block, and a price? Templates with only static "shop" imagery (a decorative product grid with no linked product page) force you to build the page template yourself.

2. Variant support. If you sell sizes, colors, or formats, confirm the product collection includes a variant field. This is the single most common gap between a "portfolio template" and a workable storefront.

3. Cart and checkout. A template should connect to Webflow's native checkout so you can customize the cart and purchase flow to match your branding. Verify the demo actually has a live cart/checkout path and uses Webflow ecommerce rather than a third-party embed.

4. Digital vs physical delivery. For digital goods (printables, fonts, courses), confirm the template supports custom delivery options so customers get download links on the order confirmation page and email. For physical goods, check there's a shipping/shipping-regions section to work with.

5. Payment methods shown. Webflow checkout is powered by Stripe, Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal. A template that supports the native checkout inherits all of these; one that hard-codes a single legacy button does not.

6. Product CMS collections included. Buying a template that includes CMS collections for products saves the biggest chunk of setup time. If the listing doesn't say "CMS" for products, plan to build those collections yourself.

7. Sale and discount displays. If you'll run promotions, the template should have list-price vs sale-price styling built in — not just a description field you'd have to style yourself.

8. Mobile behavior of the shop pages. The landing page always looks good on mobile; product grids, filters, and checkout steps often don't. Resize the demo to phone width and click through the full purchase flow.

License and cost traps specific to storefronts

The single-site license rule from the marketplace applies to ecommerce templates too — one license per live site. If you're a freelancer building client stores, an agency license or a fresh license per client project is the difference between a cheap template and an invoice disaster. Confirm the author's stated license scope in the marketplace listing before purchasing.

Budgets are additive: template + Site plan + Ecommerce plan. The template fee is usually the smallest number of the three — choose it for structure, not for savings.

Platform support you should expect

Webflow ecommerce handles order management and refunds in the dashboard, automatic sales tax and VAT for the US, Canada, the EU, and Australia, and connects to fulfillment and accounting via native integrations and Zapier (Printful, Shippo, QuickBooks, Mailchimp, and others). Use this list as your minimum bar: a template that fights any of these (e.g., a checkout that can't show digital delivery) is the wrong template, no matter how it looks.
